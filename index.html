?For businesses: already have a hauler, or two separate vendors?+

Most small offices, clinics, studios, and shops we talk to are handling old electronics one of two ways. Either a general junk hauler picks them up and charges a bulky-item minimum with a surcharge on top for electronics, or the job gets split between two vendors: one that destroys the data and another that recycles the hardware. Both cost more than they should, and neither one specializes in e-waste.

Take a typical office turnover: six laptops, two monitors, a printer, a retired server, and a box of phones, keyboards, and cables.

Example Commercial Pickup:
A general junk hauler
~$150–350+: franchise haulers set a job minimum before they load anything, quote on site so you don’t know the real number until the crew is already standing in your office, and add a disposal surcharge for electronics. Nothing gets wiped, and anything still working loses its resale value.
A national e-waste or ITAD company
Free, if you qualify: most set a business minimum before they’ll dispatch a truck, commonly around 500 lbs or roughly 10 qualifying items. A small office rarely clears that bar, so you’re either turned away or quoted a fee to come out for a partial load, and then you wait for their route to reach you.
Two vendors: shredding, then recycling
Two invoices, two visits: shredding companies generally won’t take computers, monitors, or consumer electronics, and they want drives pulled out of the machines before they arrive. So the data goes one place and the hardware goes another. In Boulder that’s two addresses across town, both open weekday business hours, which means somebody on your team is off the clock loading a car and standing in line.

Meet Our Team :)
Hi, I'm Natalie (Founder & Chief Rescue Officer)
20 Years Old · Born in Dallas, TX

I'm a junior at CU Boulder studying economics and political science. During my first semester of sophomore year, I took an Environmental Sustainability and Features course, where I learned about the serious effects of e-waste on our environment when electronics are disposed of improperly.

After researching what options existed in Boulder, I found a real gap. People are left choosing between hauling all their e-waste to a drop-off facility themselves and paying processing fees, or searching to find the best-priced general recycler or junk management service that doesn't even specialize in e-waste management to come pick it up. E-waste is our sole focus, because it's the fastest-growing solid waste stream on earth. I started Juett Tech Rescue to fill that gap: a simple, transparently priced pickup with certified e-waste recycling, and electronics with refurbishment potential refurbished, resold, or donated instead of shredded, built for Boulder residents. I established the business in December 2025, spent the winter building the website and refining the idea, and launched pickup operations in March 2026. We are proud to be the only waste management service in Boulder focusing specifically on electronic waste management.

Everything we do is transparently and fairly priced, and it's built to handle the whole process in one go. If it runs on a battery or plugs in, we pick it up, securely wipe any data that needs wiping, and take everything to a certified processing facility.

Juett Tech Rescue's goal is to save all electronic waste. As an e-waste business, we could have built our model around refurbishable items only, cherry-picking the valuable ones to chase profits and leaving the rest as something we don't take. That would completely defeat the purpose. Less than 23% of e-waste is formally recycled globally, it is the fastest-growing municipal waste stream in the world, and it accounts for roughly 70% of the toxicity in landfills when it ends up there. So we take all of it, and our motto says it plainly: if it plugs in or runs on a battery, we'll take it. This is about the full cycle of recycling, and it starts with keeping electronics out of the landfill. We still handle refurbishment; it's one part of the mission, not the whole point. What we want is to contribute to the circular economy, putting rare earth minerals back into use, whether that is through certified recycling facilities or through refurbishment that gives a device a second life by donation or resale. Not waste, but a resource worth recovering.

Electronics with refurbishment potential are restored and then donated or resold, your choice. Choosing resale puts a little money back in your pocket and takes the hassle of repairing and reselling off your hands. We do this through a specialized network: resourcing to our main partner, DRM Worldwide, along with a former CU professor who helps us donate old laptops.

The facts are striking: the world generates over 50 million metric tons of electronic waste every year, making it the fastest-growing solid waste stream on the planet. According to the Global E-Waste Monitor 2024, only 22.3% of e-waste is formally recycled globally. When electronics end up in landfills, toxic materials such as lead, mercury, and cadmium leach into the soil and groundwater, posing serious risks to both human health and local ecosystems. The World Health Organization has identified improper e-waste disposal as a growing public health concern.

The goal of this business is to make electronic waste recycling accessible for Boulder County residents and small businesses by tackling the issue head-on and closing the gap in convenience and affordability. I am also committed to giving back: a percentage of profits from every pickup is donated to Basel Action Network (BAN), a nonprofit dedicated to fighting illegal e-waste dumping and advocating for responsible recycling worldwide.
